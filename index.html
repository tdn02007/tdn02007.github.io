About

I am a Ph.D. candidate at DGIST, working with Inkyu Moon in the Intelligent Imaging and Vision Systems Lab on generative models for biological imaging.

My research develops diffusion, flow matching, and adversarial models that learn cross-modal maps between label-free images and fluorescence readouts of single cells, mostly from unpaired or weakly paired data. I also build the platform these models are trained on: a custom high-throughput quantitative phase (digital holographic) microscope, so I generate the single-cell imaging data I model.

Before this, I completed my B.S. at DGIST, and from 2021 to 2024 I was a researcher at the DGIST Research Center of Robotics and Mechatronics Engineering.

I am interested in problems at the intersection of generative modeling, computational imaging, and cell biology: learning cross-modal maps without paired ground truth, image-based phenotyping, and predicting how cells respond to perturbations. I have also worked on learning under severe measurement constraints, including photon-limited imaging and privacy-preserving federated learning.

I am currently seeking postdoctoral positions in AI for biological imaging.

Research

Cross-modal generation

Predicting stained readouts from label-free images

A single label-free measurement carries enough information to predict what a stain would have shown. I train generative models that map label-free cell images to fluorescence readouts, including virtual three-dimensional confocal fluorescence, so that molecular-scale contrast is recovered without staining, scanning, or a second optical path.

Microsystems & Nanoengineering (in press, 2026) · Laser & Photonics Reviews (under review) · Eng. Appl. Artif. Intell. (2025)
Learning without paired data

Unpaired and weakly paired distribution translation

Paired ground truth is the bottleneck in cross-modal biological imaging, because the two modalities usually cannot be measured on the same cell at the same instant. I use unsupervised and cyclic diffusion formulations that learn the mapping from unpaired populations, which makes the models portable across samples, instruments, and acquisition setups.

IEEE Trans. Multimedia (under review) · Eng. Appl. Artif. Intell. (2026) · Opt. Lasers Eng. (2023) · Biomed. Opt. Express (2021)
Phenotyping & perturbation

From single-cell morphology to biological readouts

Image-based morphological profiling that resolves biologically meaningful state: drug-response and cardiotoxicity phenotypes in treated cardiomyocytes, dexamethasone effects on young versus aged skeletal muscle, storage lesion grading in stored red blood cells, and stain-free classification of cancer cells and circulating tumor cells.

Biosens. Bioelectron. (2022) · IEEE J. Biomed. Health Inform. (2022) · Bioinformatics (2025) · Comput. Methods Programs Biomed. (2025) · Adv. Sensor Res. (2025)
Scale and constraint

Learning when measurements are scarce or cannot be shared

Label-free acquisition is cheap enough to generate single-cell corpora at the scale generative models need. I also study the opposite regime: what a model can learn when photons are scarce, or when biomedical data cannot leave the site it was collected at, via photon-limited classification and privacy-preserving federated learning.
